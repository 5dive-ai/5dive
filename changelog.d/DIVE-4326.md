## Unreleased — fix(task): a held merge is owed by ops, not by the constant `main` (DIVE-4326)

`_merge_disp_decide` printed `hold:main:<why>` in every branch, so `merge_owner`
read `main` on **every** graded-and-waiting row. That was a constant, not a
decision anyone made — and the heartbeat's DIVE-4206 rule ("a row whose next move
is someone else's is not runnable") then made those rows dispatchable to `main`
**alone**: the most expensive window on the box (5-min cadence, accumulating
context, one merge per wake). lodar, 2026-09-11: *"why you merging? shouldn't this
be ops job? why you on hands now?"*

The pure decider now stops naming a seat at all. `merger` joins `maker` as a ROLE
in the disposition's vocabulary, and both are resolved to a seat exactly once, on
the impure side — `maker` from the row, `merger` from the repo and the roster:

- **ops** by default;
- **main** only where ops cannot reach — a repo outside the owners ops's
  credential covers, or a roster in which ops is absent or its heartbeat is
  disabled (a seat the heartbeat will not wake is not an owner; a row naming one
  is dispatchable to nobody, DIVE-4220).

An unresolved repo slug is *not* evidence ops cannot read it, so the whole
transient-read family (`pr-state-unreadable`, `repo-unresolved`) stays with ops;
an unreadable roster fails **open** to ops for the same reason — re-pinning every
merge onto main when a file cannot be read is the constant this change removes,
reintroduced as a failure mode.

Unchanged, and now the full order: the **grader** keeps the row when the branch is
auto-mergeable at the graded sha (DIVE-3474 already lets a grader merge its own
PASS), then ops, then main. The picker's DIVE-4206 skip and the "GRADED AND THE
MERGE IS YOURS" wake read the same `merge_owner` column they always did, so both
follow the new owner with no second expression to keep in step.
