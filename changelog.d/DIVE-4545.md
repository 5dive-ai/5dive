## Unreleased — fix(agent): the memory index's last-resort rung WEIGHS its output instead of estimating it (DIVE-4545)

`_pack_atoms_index` degrades full → slim → sampled to keep a seeded `MEMORY.md` under the
16,000-byte always-loaded budget, past which the loader drops the TAIL with no error. The first
two rungs measured the bytes they wrote. The third **estimated** them — `shown * 80` — and the
estimate was wrong in both directions at once. Real slug lines are ~135 B, so at 400 atoms the cap
bound far too late and the file plateaued at ~26.7 kB (1.67×); and below ~190 atoms the cap never
bound at all, so the "sample" was the entire list the slim rung had just rejected, plus a preamble
and a footer — **strictly larger than the form it degraded from** (16.8 kB at 120 atoms). Both
reinstate exactly the silent tail-cut the writer exists to prevent, and nothing reded, because the
36 arms graded which form was chosen and what it said. Nothing weighed the artefact.

Measured on the real store it will meet: `/home/agent-codex/.codex/memories` (143,463 B) converts
to 139 atoms, and `origin/main` writes a 20,573 B index for it — 1.29× the budget, tail cut. The
same store on this branch writes 15,894 B and names 103 of 139, with the remainder disclosed.

The rung now renders its fixed parts, measures them, and admits a line only while the ACTUAL byte
total still fits, with two declared shrink levers (fewer lines, then shorter lines) and a NAMED
failure — a warning and a non-zero return — if not even one entry fits. Lengths are counted in
BYTES: the budget the loader enforces is in bytes and this file is full of em dashes, so under a
UTF-8 locale `${#s}` under-reads by 2 B per dash exactly the text that overflows. The slim rung
now also reserves its own omission note, which the old comparison left out.

Same file: `--format=agents-md` over a **raw codex store** rendered whole documents — 45,994 B in
one file, one section per FILE — under a header claiming "Distilled persona memory, one fact per
section". Distilled is the one thing raw mode is not. The header is now routed on the pack's
declared `includes.memoryShape`; a pack written before that field existed still reads as atoms.
