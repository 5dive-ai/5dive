## Unreleased — fix(task-engine): re-check a spooled nag against the board at delivery time (DIVE-4295)

A task-engine nag was true when it was written and false when it was read. A send
to a BUSY seat is spooled (DIVE-4214) and drains one message per idle observation,
so for a queued message "send time" is the **flush**, not the sweep. Measured on the
verifier seat 2026-09-11: 18 messages spooled between 07:20 and 08:55, among them a
nag to grade a row that had been `done` and merged for fifty minutes, and one telling
a seat that "grading is genuinely yours again" for a row it had already graded and
bounced back to the maker. Each arrival costs the recipient a full re-investigation
of work they had closed out.

The sending rail's own condition is now recorded at enqueue in a `<id>.guard`
sidecar and **re-run against the board at the moment the seat reads the message**;
`a2a_queue_flush_one` discards instead of typing. Three conditions:
`verifier_unacked` (the delivered-and-unacknowledged nag), `verifier_owns`, and
`assignee_owns` (the grader-pool wake, which is assignment-keyed because a grading
session runs on a pool seat rather than on the row's `verifier` column).

**No sidecar means unconditional**, so every pre-existing a2a caller is byte-identical,
and an unreadable board fails **open** — the sweep stamps its throttle column at
enqueue, so a wrong drop silences that rail for that row permanently while a wrong
delivery costs one re-investigation. Dropping is looped where delivery stays
one-per-call: the one-per-call rule exists because typing a second message lands it
inside the turn the first started, and a drop starts no turn.
